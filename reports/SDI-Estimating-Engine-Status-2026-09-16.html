Parity — what we can prove, and what we cannot

What it is. The engine's workbook read against the estimator's own completed sheet, block by block. Both sides are reconciled to their own totals before anything is compared, differences are signed in both directions, and the inputs behind a money gap — a blank, a gauge, a quantity, a nest — are reported beside it. It files a ledger row per job, so the picture builds rather than being re-argued each time.

When it runs. From the portal against a finished pair, or at run time where the estimator attaches the manual sheet up front.

Where it stands on the eleven: no cell-level comparison is filed for any of them, because no manual sheet has been attached to a run. The ledger in source control holds one row, from a July job. Saying otherwise would be the one thing this programme does not do — the proof is the log, not the theory.

So the parity evidence for this fortnight is the estimators' own verdicts rather than a computed variance, and there are two of them:

  • Tim on 11350-02: "Costs come out really close." His words, and the closest thing to a parity verdict on a current job.
  • 12349-02: the three defects his review found were fixed the same day and every line was then reconciled to his sheet. It is held as a replay gate, so no later change can quietly move it.
  • The other nine: no verdict either way. That is not a claim of accuracy and should not be read as one.

What turns this into a table. One command a job on the laptop, against the manual sheet wherever one exists:

python src/parity_run.py --ai output/json/<job>.json --manual <manual sheet>

Each run files a ledger row, and the eleven stop being an impression. Where a job was estimated by the engine first, the comparison can be added when the manual sheet arrives.

Why no figure off a manual sheet appears here. By rule, a number from a manual estimate is not retained in our register, documentation or reports. Parity is therefore reported as a variance and a direction, never by printing the estimator's own amount.

What must never inherit

Historic estimates teach; they do not supply prices. “A number copied from an estimator’s sheet is not a price source, even as a reference.” “A rate taken from a single estimate is a scoped pilot, never generic, until a second independent job or the department itself confirms it.”
  • Harrods 01 plating — the quoted figure is retained nowhere. What it taught lives on as a rule: Harrods 01 is decorative plating that needs a fresh, identified plater quote for each job; until one exists the line is not priced.
  • Plater freight — the reference figure from Howard's review is retained nowhere; a plated route still carries a freight line, raised awaiting a current transport quote (owner: SDI transport) on every job, 7332-01 included. The route inherits; the money does not.
  • Edging rate and tape roll price — off the estimators' sheets, so no longer held anywhere: the edging specification, the roll length and the length-arithmetic carry forward; the money comes from SDI Live or a supplier source or the line is explicitly awaiting price.
  • Held to by test, not by intent — the register, the price file, the documentation, the reports, the prompts and the audit payload are all empty of these figures, and a test asserts that a new run cannot discover or display a historic manual-estimate amount anywhere. Mechanism tests use invented figures so they prove the arithmetic without carrying a real one.
