What moved since 29 September

The server is ordered, and the go-live date is Monday 2 November. Jack Calow and James Gray placed the order with Scan 3XS this week on a fourteen-day lead time. A wider decision on how far to take the in-house Vault against PDM Standard is now put to Matt in section 07.

Client Briefing went in front of Matt and Jonathan. Sage X3 is loading the estimating tables this week: BOMs, routes and products, with customers and suppliers beside them, and estimating, customer services, finance and the warehouse as the first user groups once the first pass is in test. The 30 October target for every area loaded and tested holds.

The same lesson twice: a price somebody already has

On 1176-02 the customer's own vinyl price, and on 12567-01 the supplier's graphic price, each outweighed the whole of the rest of the variance. Nothing in a drawing pack tells the engine those prices exist. Two things follow and both are in section 07 with owners and dates: the supplier price lists, for which estimating's contact spreadsheet is now in hand, and a place on the run page for a quoted price, so a figure the account manager already holds is entered once and reaches the estimate, the quote and the price history.

What landed in the engine

A brief before the first runNew · method

For 12173-02, 12696-01 and 9439-01-04 the drawings were read first and the right book written down as facts: the parts, the joints and whether they are symbols or notes, the coats, what is supplied by others, the quantity breaks. A checker scores every book against that file. A book that fails a fact is fixed in the engine and re-run; nothing is edited by hand. On 9439-01-04 a reading error in the DXF of a thin ring was found and fixed before the first run.

A weld needs the part's own evidenceNew · Tim, Dave

Weld symbols are read off each sheet's vectors; a note on the sheet is the drawing's statement; a member stated WELDED under a welded assembly is that assembly's joint, charged once; the border legend's "ALL WELDS TO BE TIG" is how a weld is made, not that one exists, and is named on the weld row beside the rate charged. A part with no evidence of its own is asked at £0, not charged. This answers Tim's spot-welding point and Dave's standing rule that nothing is priced on a near match.

The estimator's brief, typed on the portalNew · Dave

A render-only enquiry now takes a brief in a box on the run page: quantity, materials, sizes, finish, the customer. It is filed with the pack, confirmed by the service, read as stated facts, and a run whose brief was not filed is stopped. The customer named brings its commercial terms.

The break table says what the fall is made ofNew · 12696-01

Under every quantity break the sheet now shows packaging and delivery per unit, labour set-up per unit, run labour, and the unit cost with the spread money taken off, through the unit cell's own arithmetic. An estimator can see at a glance that the fall from one off is set-up and freight dividing, not the work changing.

The portal and the runnerDone

One machine runs one portal and the page warns of a second; a restarted runner releases its job and will not restart mid-job; a job folder's sub-folders are never read; a job's own numbering is accepted as the product at any level; a busy Excel is retried and the refusal note names the real cause.

Run timeNext

Each run now writes a timing table. The work to read it and re-run only what a change affects starts when the four packs out this week have been reviewed. The server is the other half of the answer, and it is not a substitute for this.

The week to 2 October, in Yogesh's report

The Design Vault handles the hard scenariosProven

Not just the core loop. In the week: an administrator cancelling another user's checkout for holiday cover, with the lock holder told and blocked from overwriting with a stale copy until they Get Latest; cross-project and library referencing, where Check Out lists the assembly and its referenced parts including those in other projects, pulls references read-only without locking, and "save a copy for this project" creates a new file rather than editing the shared source; workflow states separate from the lock (WIP and Released, with Revise returning a file to WIP and advancing the revision letter, and Publish Production recording the released revision); and a browser-only check-out and check-in by zip download with a small local agent, so laptops need no desktop install. PDM handover: the migration path is clear30 Sep

The handover meeting with Ian, Steve, James and Yogesh confirmed that PDM Standard stores its metadata in SQL with the files as an archive on disk, that SQL access is permitted, that PDM and SolidWorks versions must stay matched, and that a handover document follows. Standard has no API, but its SQL can be linked to the archive files, which de-risks a future PDM to Vault migration whichever way the October decision goes.
