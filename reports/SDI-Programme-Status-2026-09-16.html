Eleven jobs, nine named

Tim's three are now attributed: 12349-02, 11350-02 and 11350-01. Two of the eleven are still not named in the sources this report is compiled from, and the estimating page's run ledger is where that is settled. The count stands; two names are what is outstanding.

And the ask that goes with the count. Eleven jobs in two weeks is against a minimum of five a week, so the run rate is being met on our side. Several of those eleven are still awaiting a reply. The reviews are what move the go-live date, and they are worth more than another job going out: Tim's fourteen points on 12349-02 became fourteen changes, and his four on 11350-02 became four more.

The rule the reviews were held to

Roughly forty specific findings came back across the six reviews, and the discipline for every one was the same: the engine learns the method, the route and the evidence; it never copies a figure off an estimator's sheet. A number from a manual estimate is not a price source, even as a reference, and is retained nowhere — not in the register, the documentation, the reports, the prompts, the tests or the audit record. A price comes from SDI Live, a supplier catalogue or an identified current quote, or the line says plainly that it is awaiting one and who owns it. Written into the change register as its non-negotiable pricing policy, with a test that a new run cannot discover or display a historic manual amount anywhere.

Parity — what we can prove, and what we cannot

Parity is the engine's workbook read against the estimator's own completed sheet, block by block, each side reconciled to its own totals before anything is compared, differences signed both ways, filing a ledger row per job. It runs from the portal against a finished pair, or at run time where the manual sheet is attached up front.

No cell-level comparison is filed for any of the eleven

Because no manual sheet has been attached to a run. The ledger in source control holds one row, from a job in July. Claiming parity on this fortnight would be the one thing this programme does not do — the proof is the log, not the theory — so what follows is the estimators' own verdicts rather than a computed variance.

  • Tim on 11350-02: "Costs come out really close." His words, and the closest thing to a parity verdict on a current job.
  • 12349-02: the three defects his first review found were fixed the same day and every line was then reconciled to his sheet. It is held as a replay gate, so no later change can quietly move it.
  • The other nine: no verdict either way, and that is not a claim of accuracy.

What turns this into a table: one command a job on the laptop against the manual sheet wherever one exists. Each run files a ledger row, and where the engine estimated first, the comparison is added when the manual sheet arrives. Ask: the manual sheets for the eleven.

Why no figure off a manual sheet appears in this report. By rule a number from a manual estimate is not retained in the register, the documentation or the reports, so parity is reported as a variance and a direction and never by printing an estimator's own amount.

What landed in the engine

One workbook, every quantityNew · James

An enquiry asked at 10, 50, 250 and 1,000 now produces one spreadsheet rather than one per quantity. The estimators' own Material Price Break tab is filled by the engine and a Quantity Breaks tab recalculates the whole estimate at each quantity, with order-level freight divided by that quantity instead of a pallet landed on every unit. "Still generating multiple s/sheets" — 15 Sep. The break tab was already doing its job in the first workbook; the old per-quantity mechanism was still filing copies underneath it. The sweep still computes the figures the tab and the report read — it simply stops filing the copies.

Every quantity on one workbookNew · Howard

The estimators' own Material Price Break tab is filled by the engine, and a Quantity Breaks tab recalculates the same estimate at every quantity the enquiry asks for — 10, 50, 250 and 1,000 on the graphic holder — with the formulas showing, as Howard asked. The base sheet is run at one unit deliberately, so each department's set-up stands visible rather than hidden inside a volume average.

Roll goods are priced by the length usedNew · Howard

Tape, edging and anything else sold by the metre is priced by the length cut off the roll, and the roll price is asked of SDI Live on each run. Howard's tape had been three strips at three pack prices. Generic: it fires on every job with a roll-good, not on his.

One laser row per component, one set-up per proven sheetNew · Howard

Each laser-cut component gets its own labour row at its own rate, as the estimators' sheets do. Set-up is shared only where the nesting proves the components sit on one stock sheet — the proof is the engine's own nest arithmetic, and where it fails each component carries its own set-up. On the Harrods stand's second run, the five rows landed; the shared set-up on two of them did not, and is on the open list.

What the shop knows that the drawing does not sayNew · Howard

Five production rules, each a confirmed decision with the estimator's name and date on it, each visible on the line it changes and overridable by an estimator: production runs 1.0 mm steel where a drawing calls for 0.9; a mitred square-tube leg is sawn, not bent; welds are dressed or not by the customer's own standard (M&S yes, TTI no); a plated part is packed out and packed back as two operations; brushing before plating is a named operation. A rule of this kind cannot be outvoted by the drawing readers, which is what had kept the 0.9 on one part.

The joinery route existsNew · Tony

Saw and spindle work minted from the drawing's own words (a rebate gets the spindle); a board assembly gets its bench-fitting line; the edging specification is held as a specification with its rate from the supplier catalogue; and every department's set-up is separated from its run time — a convention Tony's sheet and Howard's both confirm, so it is shop-wide. A guard is queued for Tony's next jobs: a purchased, customer-supplied or pre-assembled board parent must not be given bench work merely for having child records.

An unpriced line says whose gap it isNew · Tim

Three lines on Tim's comms bar read "NOT PRICED — needs a rate", and his reply asked the question that wording had failed to answer: is the spec missing from the drawing, or can the system not find a cost? They were not one case. Two carried a code the engine had minted from the description because the pack gives none for a wing nut or a PEM stud — nothing can be looked up against a code we wrote, and the placeholder sits in the code column looking like a part number. The third carried DBR60, a real code, which was asked and came back with nothing. Each line now says which, because the answers differ: one is closed by a code on the pack, the other by loading the purchasing catalogue. Fires on the shape of the line, so it reaches every job with a bought item.

Underneath it, the standing item. No supplier price list is loaded into the purchasing catalogue yet, so a bought line with a perfect spec still finds an empty shelf. Elite, Eagle and Thermaset remain the action.

Where a figure came from, in arithmetic that reproduces itNew · Tim

Tim asked where the pemming rate and the powder cost come from. Both are now answered on the record rather than in an e-mail. Pemming is the MANM department rate against 15 seconds a pressed fastener — which is his own 12120 sheet read back, where 4 clinch nuts at 60 units an hour and 2 PEM studs at 120 both come to 240 inserts an hour. Powder is priced by weight and the colour never enters the sum: coated area, times a coverage figure, times the per-kilo rate. In both cases the stated constants reproduce the booked row exactly, which is the test a figure has to pass before it is allowed to stand.

Three figures go back to him to rule, because each came off one sheet or is our own derivation: the per-kilo powder rate, the coverage (we hold one figure, the workbook template's own calculator holds another a fifth apart), and the 15 seconds. His proposal to put the powder reference on the drawing is accepted in principle — it prices the line from a real SKU instead of an average and retires two of the three questions.

The change register is the operating contractDone

Every rule above is a numbered decision, D-016 to D-082, recording who ruled it, on which job, whether it is generic or a scoped pilot from one job, where it is implemented and the test that proves it. It is the hand-off document for the estimators: what the engine believes, why, and who said so — so the same question is never asked twice.

Why it matters. Proof is the log, not the theory. A change that only fires on the job it was found on does not ship.

The Harrods stand's second run, assessed 16 SeptemberIn progress

Landed: five laser rows per component on template rates, weld and dress times as Howard stated them, the pack-to-plater rows, plating and freight carried as awaiting quotes. Not yet: the shared laser set-up on two nested parts, the brushing minutes on the frame, the pack minutes on the frame, a tube-bend flag still on the sawn leg, and the plating specification not identified on the run. One of these was diagnosed and fixed from the book alone; the rest need the run's own record from the laptop, which has been asked for.

After the parallel run — unchanged from 7 September

The automated pipeline and on-demand controls stand as described last time: one button imports the full and latest drawing pack, one click generates the estimate with its explains and e-mails it with the quote, parity at the time of the run or added afterwards. In now: amend the estimate and regenerate the quote; print the pack from the portal. No timetable yet for China quotations or multi-PDF packs. Nothing here moved this period; the period went on the reviews.

Drawing Search is live, out of UAT, and on the portalLive

Live for the design and technical teams. It searches 32,422 drawings by what is actually printed on the sheet — the client, the material, the finish, the RAL, the job number, a line from a note — and from the same screen a designer opens the SolidWorks or 3ds Max file straight into the software rather than hunting through folders. Ian tested it and liked it, which is why it went in. Muhammad's fixes this week all came from watching real use: opening the right drawing, and not hiding drawings filed in unusual places — this year's Boots Dynamix drawings were not showing at all and now are.

It has been added to the SDI Intelligence portal as SDI Drawing Search Intelligence, directly below its user guide, on the laptop and on the server. The IIS/nginx production home with AD sign-in and the index on the K: drive remains the plan; the address on the menu is the one to use until then.

Next. More designers on it on real jobs. The index grows as drawings are filed, and a drawing filed in an unusual place is a bug to fix, not a limitation to accept. The 7 September "no buffer on go-live" risk is closed by the go-live.

It is not yet integrated with SDI IntelligenceNext

The search runs as its own web application. The portal links to it rather than containing it, and the two systems do not yet share anything but a menu entry. Joining them is what turns a drawing found in the library into a part code the price chain already understands — the point at which finding the fixture and costing it become one action rather than two.

Stated as a gap rather than left implied, because the menu entry makes it look joined up already.
