What one axis shows that three trackers did not

Four go-lives inside five weeks. Technical Design P1 on 15 Sep and P2 on 24 Sep, Drawing Search on 25 Sep, and the estimating engine targeting September / October. Four releases into the same business, from three owners who do not share a plan, inside the same month — and two of them land on consecutive days.

The first go-live is not the one everybody is watching. Drawing Search rolls out on 25 Sep, ahead of the estimating engine that every status report leads with.

Nothing blocks anything — worth stating plainly, because it is the good news. No workstream waits on another, so a slip in one does not move the others. The exposure is not dependency, it is attention: the same estimators and designers are the users for more than one of these, and all four land at once.

Deterministic cost-estimating engine — SolidWorks PDF and DXF drawings in, a costed five-tab workbook out, mirroring the manual estimating workbook to the cell. All 80 workbook formulas reverse-engineered; a growing store of historical SDI jobs feeds comparables on material, labour and operations.

Where it is

In parallel run. The engine produces the workbook; the parity report compares it to the manually completed one cell by cell. What has changed this period is mostly about trust — several things reported success when they had not succeeded, and those now fail honestly.

A run that produces no workbook now says soFixed

A job ran for sixteen minutes, reported done, and produced nothing. One missing template file silently took out the estimate, the quote, the job report, the Decision Report and the AI Provenance sheet at once, while the runner still reported success. The run now fails with an explanation, and the template is checked before the run starts.

The parity report shows what it actually comparedFixed

The HTML report was not always written; where it was, the variance column was calculated from a different pair of numbers than the difference column beside it. It is now written on every run, and every figure comes from the same source as the figure next to it.

Three files per comparison, not two — and the HTML one is the one to read.

The price chain, stated as it is wiredDocumented

Seven sources, strictly ordered, first hit wins — no scoring across sources. UDEF parts master (exact code 0.95, fuzzy 0.82) → ERP parts master (purchased items, 0.55–0.88) → bought-in catalogue → historical comparables → supplier catalogue → standard commodity → web / LLM (capped 0.68).

The first six are reproducible; the seventh is not, which is why it is last and capped. A zero is never a price at any step.

A category word was being used as a part codeFixed

Raised by Tim. He can find FIXING41, FIXING513 and FIXING1122 in the parts master; he cannot find FIXING, because that is the word for what the thing is. The engine was asking the catalogue for it as though it were a code — which in the ERP parts master would have returned one price for every generic fixing on every drawing, at the highest confidence the chain can express. Class words are now refused as identifiers, and the line prices from its description.

Two things closed todayDone

The SDILive password is rotated and verified against the live catalogue, and the credential is out of the source tree — it had been a literal in seventeen tracked files, with the environment file itself tracked by git, so "put the new password in .env only" would have committed it straight back into the same exposure.

A permanent SolidWorks seat is installed on the estimating laptop. That unblocks four finished tools in Workstream 2, including the DXF flat-pattern generation this engine most needs — see below.

What it is, in one paragraph

Two threads. First the Document Manager tools — they read SolidWorks project data without needing a SolidWorks seat, and pull the right drawing pack for a job straight into the estimating app and into the shop-floor Production folder. Then an SDI in-house PDM, in four phases, giving engineering proper file management, revision control and release workflows.

This is not integration with the SOLIDWORKS PDM vault. SDI is building its own, and the Document Manager tools read the live CAD share read-only. Getting that wrong changes what the work is.

Built on GitLab and LFS with a Document Manager intelligence layer over PostgreSQL. Around 50 requirements captured from design, engineering and directors; the register is still open.

Why it runs into January, and it is not the amount of work

There is one developer. Requirement collection, development, unit testing, peer review, deployment and go-live are all hands-on and have to run one at a time — only user testing overlaps, because users do that. Add leave from 10 December to 4 January and the last two PDM phases land in the new year.

The dates are a capacity constraint, not an estimate of effort. If they need to come forward, the lever is a second pair of hands, not a tighter plan.

Four finished tools were held by a licence — the seat is now installedChanged

These were never planned work. They are built and demoed to Ed Cooper and Ian B, and every one carried go-live subject to SolidWorks licence. A permanent seat is now on the estimating laptop, so the constraint is lifted there; the remaining question is which machines get a seat, not whether the tools can run.

  • C1 · Multi-format export pack — one click from a finished design to DXF flat patterns, PDF and DWG drawings, STEP per part and IGES per body.
  • C2 · Design validation suite — topple, weight, interference and cross-part hole alignment in one pass. Topple checked on a live assembly at ~25.9°.
  • C3 · Drawing generation — views, sheets, BOM and title block from a finished model; designers add the critical dimensions.
  • C4 · Machine tolerance checks — against the real TRUMPF tool list and SDI standards. Still in proof of concept.

Why C1 matters most. The estimating engine's single largest source of inaccuracy is drawings arriving without flat patterns — with no DXF it has to infer geometry, and that is where the wrong numbers come from. C1 generates them, and it works today.

Three overlaps worth knowing about before they bite

  • Drawing Search — UAT starts before the build it is testing is finished. UAT runs 5–25 Sep; development round 2 runs to 15 Sep and integration round 2 to 20 Sep. Studio designers are on a build still being hardened, so early feedback may be against code that changes underneath them.
  • Drawing Search — go-live is the day UAT ends. ~25 Sep, nothing between sign-off and studio-wide rollout. Anything found in the final week lands on the rollout date.

Client Briefing also runs unit & integration testing (15–25 Oct) and UAT (15–30 Oct) concurrently rather than in sequence — a deliberate compression on a client-facing release, worth knowing as the plan rather than discovering as a slip.

What it actually does, since the name does not say

Image and drawing search for the design studio. CLIP-based visual search with PDF indexing over roughly 268,000 images, GPU-accelerated with a CPU fallback, behind a Flask UI with a scan cache. SDI has drawn the same bracket, the same shelf and the same plinth many times over, and until now the only index of that was what a designer happened to remember.

It is not the estimating engine's description matching pointed at a different table. That scores word overlap between two pieces of text; this searches the images, which is why it can find a fixture whose description never said what it looked like. The two may meet later — a drawing found here carries a part code, and a part code is a key the price chain already understands — but they are separate pieces of work today.

05 Open across the programme

Ordered by what it costs to leave alone. Recorded here rather than left to be rediscovered.

Read the balance of this list carefully

Most of what follows sits on Workstream 1, and that is not a statement about which workstream is in the best shape. It reflects how each is reported: Workstream 1 is reported from its own source code, where a defect can be found by looking; Workstreams 2 and 3 are reported from delivery trackers, which record phases and dates rather than faults. The two are not comparable, and a workstream with fewer entries here has not been shown to have fewer problems — only fewer that this method can see.

A published API secret has never been rotatedAct

The BrightHR client secret has been in the estimating repository's history since its first commit in April, and the repository was public for four months. It is now private and the environment files are untracked — neither of those undoes history. Anyone who has ever cloned it still has the value. Rotating the credential is the only cure. The SDILive database password has been rotated and verified; this one has not.

Workstream 1.

The new database password is not yet on the serverOpen

The laptop runs on the rotated login, verified against the live catalogue. SDI-APP01's two environment files still carry the old value and the service needs restarting after they are updated. Worth checking the SQL error log for failed logins a day or two afterwards, to find any consumer nobody remembered.

Workstream 1. One trap: the password is pasted into an ODBC connection string, where a semicolon ends the value and starts a new keyword — the server then answers "Login failed", which reads as the wrong password rather than an unusable one. The engine now refuses such a password up front and names the character.

The server cannot report which build it is runningOpen

The health check shows commit: unknown on SDI-APP01, and parity runs there need one setting pointed at the right Python. A configuration script fixing both is written and not yet run on the server. This is the one host where two portals can drift apart, which is what the field exists to catch.

Workstream 1.

Three costing faults on the Dyson packOpen

  • Cross-job contamination — a fixing from an unrelated fragrance-cabinet job was costed onto a Dyson estimate. The test guarding this was found to be vacuous and removed; the guard itself needs rebuilding.
  • Prose read as a part number — a draughtsman's name and date in a title block was read as a part, given a material from a revision note saying the panel had been removed, and costed anyway.
  • Powder coating at £0.00 on a powder-coated job. The rate is settled — £9.73/kg, evidenced from the manual sheets on jobs 1303A and 1304; a second £12.50 constant was traced and is dead code with no callers. What remains open is policy: whether an unstated finish defaults to powder with a visible assumption flag, or is a required estimator decision.
Workstream 1.

Two price sources are wired to tables that are emptyOpen

Rung 3 of the price chain reads the bought-in catalogue where rows are active. A migration moved that catalogue to a new table and set every migrated row inactive, so the rung returns nothing — the successor holds 44 rows. And the table where a priced estimate is meant to be saved does not exist: no run has ever recorded which source won a price.

Workstream 1. Two small decisions: repoint rung 3 at the successor table, and either create the results table or remove the save.

Size is not weighted in description matchingOpen

Historical price matching scores word overlap and has no idea which word is the size, so a wrong-size line of the same family can outscore the right part written with different punctuation. Recorded as a deliberately failing test so it cannot be forgotten. Fixing it changes matching for every priced line, so it wants doing on its own.

Workstream 1.

Supplier price lists are not loadedOpen

The ingester is built and tested — it turns a supplier's spreadsheet into catalogue rows, reads the unit from the column then the price heading then the description and says which of the three it used, and refuses a file that is not a price list rather than half-parsing one. It needs the actual files: Elite (Tim Allen), Eagle (Cameron) and Thermaset (Donna).

Workstream 1.

Drawing Search go-live has no bufferOpen

Studio-wide rollout on 25 Sep is the same day UAT ends, and UAT overlaps both remaining build phases. This is a schedule shape rather than a defect, and it is the single most likely place for the programme's first go-live to slip.

Workstream 3. A decision for the owner, not a fix.

Workstream 2 has one developer and no coverOpen

Requirement collection, development, unit testing, peer review, deployment and go-live are all hands-on and run one at a time; only user testing overlaps. Leave from 10 December to 4 January sits inside the PDM phases. Every date from P3.1 onwards depends on one person being available, and there is no second pair of hands to move work to.

Workstream 2. A capacity constraint, not an estimate of effort — which means it cannot be recovered by re-planning.

The PDM requirement register is still openOpen

Around 50 requirements have been captured from design, engineering and directors, and the register has not been closed. The four PDM phases carry dates against a scope that can still grow.

Workstream 2.

The indexing finding has not yet been re-verifiedOpen

Integration testing round 1 found the Renders folders were not indexed — roughly 162,000 of 268,000 images. Round 2 (15–20 Sep) has to confirm all ~268,000 are searchable and all three models accurate. Until it does, the 25 Sep go-live rests on a fix that has been made but not yet proven, and it is the same test that caught the problem the first time.

Workstream 3.

Client Briefing is building to a document not yet signedOpen

Requirements gathering ends 21 September and development starts on the 25th. The scope document has to be agreed in those four days, and the entire build is specified by it. A week's delay in sign-off is a week off the front of a three-week build, not a week added to the end.

Workstream 3.

Which machines get a SolidWorks seatOpen

The estimating laptop now has one, which lifts the constraint on C1–C4 there. Nothing has been decided about the other machines — and it is the difference between four finished tools running for one person and running for the department.

Workstreams 1 and 2.

DWG drawings are silently ignoredParked

No converter is configured, so a DWG in a pack contributes nothing and says nothing. Parked deliberately, recorded so it is not rediscovered as a surprise.
