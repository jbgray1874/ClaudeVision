Twelve days after the August issue, the first week of September delivered the densest frontier release window of the year: Claude Fable 5.1 (1 Sep), Gemini 3.8 Flash (2 Sep), Meta Muse Spark 1.3 (2 Sep) and GPT-6 Astra (3 Sep). None of them changes the engine's architecture, and this report says so plainly. What it adds is four sections James asked for by name: G-code from DXF for the CNC workstream, drawing packs from SOLIDWORKS, the estimating market, and replicating PDM in-house — each with a verdict and a next step rather than a list.

Three findings are worth reading before the rest. The shared workstation ordered this week carries a 16 GB card, and the on-premise vision model this report recommended in August needs about 17 GB — decide that before the order goes. "AI G-code" is a milling story in 2026: every AI CAM product on the market is 3-axis or 3+2 milling, and the honest route for a TRUMPF laser is DXF into TruTops Boost's own automation, which SDI already owns. And Werk24 reads a PDF drawing into structured JSON for under a pound a page, which is the first product close enough to the engine's own readers to be worth an afternoon as a comparator.

On sourcing. Vendor entries come from the vendor's own pages. Secondary entries come from third-party write-ups and want confirming against vendor pricing before anything is committed. On the August actions: the code says the two model-string swaps have not been made — the engine still calls grok-4.3 for vision and claude-sonnet-4 on the Anthropic path — and no report back is recorded on the LEO, Anchorpoint or CADshift items. They are carried below, not repeated as new.

On-premise vision, revisited — the card in the order is 16 GB

Decide this week, before the workstation order goes

The August recommendation and the September order do not fit each other

August said: Qwen3.8-27B, 4-bit, fits a 24 GB card; price a card and benchmark it on 10575-02. The shared workstation specification of 3 September, going to order this week, carries an NVIDIA RTX PRO 2000 or RTX 2000 Ada, 16 GB — chosen for Drawing Search's 9 GB resident index and because it is SOLIDWORKS-certified. Both reasons are good. But secondary write-ups put the 4-bit 27B at about 17 GB, and that is before the vision encoder's working memory and before Drawing Search's index shares the card.

So the local-vision trial does not run on this box as specified. That is not an argument against the box; it is an argument for deciding on purpose. Three honest options, in the order I would take them:

1. Order as specified, and keep vision hosted. Vision at rank 40 costs pence a page today and the engine's determinism proof does not depend on it. Revisit when a card is needed for something else. 2. Step to a 24 GB certified card (RTX PRO 4000 class) and run the trial on the same machine — a few hundred pounds on a £4.8–5.05k request, and the PSU headroom the spec already allows for. 3. Put a consumer 24 GB card in the estimating laptop's dock or a second box and keep the certified card for SOLIDWORKS — two cards, two jobs, no contention with Drawing Search.

What moved on the local model since AugustSecondary
Qwen3.8-27B~17 GB at 4-bitPerplexity ships it locally

Two things. The official model card's agentic numbers are out (Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 63.4 to 73.0, OSWorld-Verified from 63.9 to 84.3), which matters less to us than the vision reports: it reads charts inside PDFs, and when asked for bounding boxes it wrote its own program and returned pixel coordinates, mostly correct. That is the drawing-page task in miniature.

And Perplexity now ships a post-trained Qwen3.8-27B to run on a customer's Mac as the private half of a cloud task, with a 0.6B PII classifier deciding what may leave the machine. A search company betting its privacy story on this exact model, at this exact size, is stronger evidence for the class than any benchmark table.

→ The trial plan from August stands; only the card question is new

What the trial would measure, unchanged

The same pages of one pack through llm_full_extract on grok-4.3 and through a local Qwen3.8, against the manual estimate. The pack is now 7332-01 rather than 10575-02, because 7332-01 has a saved run, a returned manual sheet and a costed record to diff against line by line.

The engine treats vision as rank 40 — it fills gaps and never overrides a DXF or a title-block read — so a worse local model cannot corrupt an estimate; it can only leave more gaps. That is what makes the trial safe, and it is why the answer is a count of gaps filled correctly, not a benchmark score.

Frontier models — four in 72 hours, and what each is for here

The engine's rule stands: deterministic readers win, models fill gaps

Claude Fable 5.1 — cheaper to remember, stricter to call1 Sep 2026
$10 / $50 per 1MCache read $0.251M contextWatermarked output

The headline is the cache: a cached input token drops from $1.00 to $0.25, which turns a long-running agent that re-reads the same context into a quarter of the bill. The three breaking changes are the part a developer has to read: forced tool choice returns HTTP 400; earlier models cannot read 5.1's thinking blocks, so a Sonnet-to-Fable escalation chain breaks; and editing an earlier turn invalidates later thinking. Outputs carry an invisible statistical watermark readable through a detection API.

For the engine: _call_anthropic_llm sends one plain user message with no tool choice, so none of the three changes apply. The August recommendation stands — the right model on that path is claude-opus-5 at half the price with the effort toggle, not Fable.

→ No change; the Opus 5 swap is the action, and it is carried from August

Gemini 3.8 Flash — the one to try on a whole PDF2 Sep 2026
$0.75 / $3.75 introBatch / Flex halfPDF input1M in · 64K out

Same price as 3.7 Flash to 31 December, then doubling. What is new for us is native PDF input with a 1M context: a whole drawing pack can go in as a document rather than page by page as rasters, with a thinking level set per call. Batch and Flex pricing at $0.375/$1.875 is the cheapest capable vision on the market for anything that can wait a few minutes.

That is the shape of the multi-PDF pack work already in the engine (working, needs testing, "against our model or purely against an LLM"). This is the purely-against-an-LLM arm of that test, and it now has a price a cost case can carry — with a January line for the doubling.

The engine's OpenAI path runs gpt-4.1-mini as a fallback for price lookups. A frontier-priced model on a fallback path is the wrong trade, and nothing GPT-6 does for drawing pages is claimed to beat the vision routes already on the shortlist. Recorded so nobody has to ask.

Grok 4.6 pricing, now published — and the swap ruleSecondary
$2 / $6 per 1M$4 / $12 above 200K500K context

Short-context rates are $2.00 input, $0.50 cached, $6.00 output; a prompt over 200K tokens doubles all three. The engine's vision prompts are single pages, nowhere near the step, so the cheap tier is the real one.

The rule for the two carried swaps, now that a parallel run is live: the five jobs a week are being compared to estimators' sheets on grok-4.3. Changing the vision model under a live batch would put a model change and a review finding in the same diff. So: make the swaps on a replay of 7332-01 from its saved JSON, confirm item counts and the costed record hold across three runs, then move the live run to the new strings between batches, one swap at a time.

→ Replay first, one swap per run, live batch untouched until it is measured

CNC automation from DXF — what "AI G-code" means in 2026, and what it does not

For the manufacturing workstream Muhammad is writing up from this month

Every AI CAM product on the market is a milling product

CloudNC CAM Assist — reinforcement learning over machining scenarios, in use at over 1,000 shops — plugs into Mastercam, Fusion, GibbsCAM, SolidCAM and Siemens NX and generates 3-axis and 3+2 milling strategies. Toolpath (Bedford, Ohio; founded 2021) quotes, checks manufacturability and programs in the browser, then hands the CAM program to Fusion — milling. Autodesk Assistant in Fusion now creates setups and generates toolpaths from natural language — milling first. None of them lists a 2D laser, punch or plasma post as the thing they automate.

For a TRUMPF laser the automation already exists and is not AI. TruTops Boost imports DXF, STEP and IGES, disassembles assemblies, and its Boost button runs nesting, tooling and sequencing to an NC program with no model in the loop — a 2026 industry assessment says so plainly: rule-based, no AI inside. Oseon sits above it with open interfaces to ERP. SDI runs this stack on SDI-APP01 today.

So the workstream's first question is not "which model writes G-code". It is: what does Boost need to receive, from where, to run its own automation without a person re-keying it — a DXF flat with bend lines on the right layers, a quantity, a material, a due date, and a route. That is exactly the record Muhammad's mapping of the live production spreadsheet produces, and exactly what C1's DXF export and the estimating engine's BOM already carry.

Where the engine already touches this

The estimating engine reads DXF geometry today for cut length, pierce count and bend count — the same geometry a laser post consumes. It does not need to become a CAM system; it needs the DXF it reads to be the DXF Boost receives, so the estimate and the NC program describe one part. C1's flat-pattern export with bend lines is that file. The production report and the actuals coming back are the other half of the loop this workstream closes.

→ Into Muhammad's BRD as questions: what Boost imports, on which layers, and the Oseon order interface

What to stop saying

The programme pages describe this workstream as "AI-generated G-code from DXF geometry, streamed to CNC machines". In 2026 that sentence is true of milling shops and not of a TRUMPF laser line, where the programming is already automatic and the gap is the order data around it. The write-up should describe the integration — DXF, quantity, material, route into Boost's order pool; actuals back out of Oseon — and leave G-code to the post-processor TRUMPF already ships. That is a smaller claim and a deliverable one.

Drawing packs from SOLIDWORKS — LEO draws by conversation from October

C3 is POC-complete in-house; the vendor is about to ship the interactive version

SOLIDWORKS 2027 lands in October with drawing generation in the box

The Preview programme ran from 15 July to mid-August; the launch event is SOLIDWORKS Live on 15 October. In 2027, LEO generates a drawing from a model: you state the template, the standard, the primary view and what the drawing must carry, refine it by prompt, and save to a location. The 3DEXPERIENCE beta it grew from produces standard and section views, hole callouts, driven dimensions, GD&T frames and datums, revision tables and BOMs for assemblies. Autodesk has the same in Fusion as Drawing Automation.

This overlaps C3 directly (drawing generation from ASM/PRT, POC-complete, waiting on seats). It does not replace it: LEO is a conversation per model; P2 needs a batch across a project folder with no one typing. The decision is which of the two SDI owns for the batch case, and whether the answer is "LEO for the designer at the desk, C3 headless for the production pack". Also unresolved: whether LEO runs on the desktop seat or needs a 3DEXPERIENCE role — the Labs tab in 2026 FD01 is desktop, so probably the former, but confirm.

LEO's other trick is still F1, and still untriedVendor

August's first action was to open SOLIDWORKS Labs on a seat we hold and try LEO's PDF-to-parametric on a legacy drawing, because F1 on the roadmap is that exact capability marked "POC pending". No result is recorded. Since then Dassault added STEP and IGES to parametric in 2026x FD02, so the reverse-engineering path now covers both ways a pack arrives without native models.

A permanent seat is now installed on the estimating laptop. The trial costs an hour.

→ Carried: one legacy PDF through LEO, on the laptop seat, result written down

The Document Manager API key moves with the releaseVendor

P1 and P2 are built on the Document Manager API, and a new API key must be requested for each major release. When designers move to SOLIDWORKS 2027, files saved from it will be read by a 2026 DM library until that key and library are updated. This is a diary item, not a risk — but it lands in the same window as P2's go-live on 29 September and the PDM MVP build, and it is cheaper to schedule than to discover.

→ On Yogesh's list: request the 2027 DM API key at release; test P1/P2 against 2027 files

The add-in route to the flat pattern, one more entryVendor

August named CADshift for batch DXF export with bend lines. SPI's TRUMPF Interface is the other one worth a price: an SDI-relevant vendor whose sheet-metal add-in unfolds in SOLIDWORKS and hands the flat directly to TruTops with bend data intact — which joins the C1 question to the CNC one above. Both need a seat, as C1 does; the question remains whether to own that export or buy it.

Estimating — the market got closer, and one product is worth an afternoon

Drawing-to-quote is now a category; nobody in it prices against SDI's own sheet

Werk24 — a PDF drawing in, structured JSON outVendor
€0.95 / page, no commitmentor €990 / month + €0.85 / page5–20 s / pagePython client

Reads a PDF or image drawing and returns JSON in three mixable SKUs: the title block (drawing ID, designation, material, weight, tolerances, projection, BOM, notes); features (dimensions, fits, threads, bores, chamfers, GD&T, radii, surface roughness, each with a confidence and x/y bounding box); and process signals (primary and secondary processes to DIN 8580, volume and bounding-box estimates, feasibility flags for quoting). CAD approximation currently for flat parts, which is to say sheet metal.

This is the first product that does what llm_full_extract and extractor_patterns do, rather than what a construction takeoff tool does. It does not price, does not know the SDI workbook, and does not resolve a GA-to-detail tree. What it offers is an independent read of the same page with a confidence per field — either a rank-40 source beside vision, or the comparator the engine has never had.

→ An afternoon: the 7332-01 pack through their API; their JSON against the costed record, field by field

Paperless Parts Wingman, and the rest of the categorySecondary
WingmanQuotationFactoryCutQuoteaPriori

Wingman reads the print — bends, welds, threads, finishes, critical callouts — and flags risk before the estimator prices; a proprietary model, from around $500 a month per shop. QuotationFactory (Netherlands) prices laser-cut sheet parts from geometry with production parameters. CutQuote turns a DXF into a laser quote in seconds — cut length and pierces, nothing after the laser. aPriori costs from a digital twin of the part for large-manufacturing buyers.

The verdict from August holds and is now sharper: the category has moved from measuring floor plans to reading fabrication prints, so the approach is validated by a market; and still nobody prices a folded, welded, plated display unit on SDI's workbook against 1,982 SDI jobs with a source on every line.

The research route, if the readers plateauSecondary

Two published systems are the fine-tune path: Florence-2 fine-tuned on 400 annotated drawings for GD&T, benchmarked against GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 on precision, recall and hallucination; and a hybrid of YOLOv11-obb oriented detection with a Donut document parser over 1,367 drawings across nine field classes (GD&T, general tolerances, measures, materials, notes, radii, roughness, threads, title blocks). Oriented boxes matter because dimensions sit at angles and title blocks stack.

Not an action. SDI's own corpus of drawings with returned manual estimates is exactly the training set these papers had to build by hand — a reason to keep the parallel run's returned sheets, not just the verdicts.

Document models, one line eachSecondary

Mistral OCR 4 returns text with bounding boxes, typed blocks (tables, titles, signatures) and inline confidences. dots.ocr parses multilingual layout in a single vision-language model. The consensus on classical tools has not moved: pdfplumber, Camelot and Docling are right for digital PDFs with borders; layout models are for scans and borderless tables. The engine's hybrid — rules for the predictable fields, a model for the rest — is still the recommended shape.

Replicating PDM in-house — three things to design in before P3.1

MVP now 6 November after the 4 September re-plan; the build case is unchanged

GitLab already gives you check-out enforcement for freeVendor
Git LFS exclusive locks.gitattributes lockableSOLIDWORKS honours read-only

Mark *.sldprt and *.sldasm as lockable in .gitattributes and Git LFS makes every local copy read-only until locked; GitLab exclusive locks hold across all branches. SOLIDWORKS warns on opening a read-only file, which is the closest a Git workflow gets to PDM-style check-out — and it is the exact behaviour P3.1's "check-in/out and locking" describes.

This is the cheapest decision on the PDM: adopt LFS locks as the mechanism in the MVP rather than writing a lock table, and spend the effort on the part nothing sells — the Document Manager intelligence layer that knows what is inside the file.

→ P3.1 design note: LFS exclusive locks are the lock; the intelligence layer sits above them

Anchorpoint, read as prior artVendor

Locks a thousand files in under a second through its own metadata server, which can be self-hosted; cleans Git LFS caches to keep storage down; sparse checkout for TB-scale repositories; SOLIDWORKS handled without configuration; monthly releases. August asked for an hour on how it handles binary churn; no result is recorded. The LFS-cache clean-up is their answer to the storage half of it — prune unreferenced objects on a schedule — and it is the one to copy.

→ Carried: an hour on Anchorpoint's churn and locking model before P3.1 starts

Aras Innovator Community Edition — free, and not the answerVendor

No licence fee up to 50 named users. But the SOLIDWORKS connector is Elysium's, sold separately; the 3D visualisation release supports SOLIDWORKS to 2025; and it is enterprise PLM with a configuration surface nobody at SDI would own. Worth reading for its data model — parts, documents, CAD structure, where-used — before P3.2 designs ours. Not worth installing.

The binary-churn question, restated for November

August's warning stands: a SOLIDWORKS assembly changes at the binary level on every open-and-save, so a naive LFS history grows with activity rather than with change. With P3.1 moved to 6 November there is more time to answer it, not less reason: gate commits on explicit check-in rather than on save, content-hash the meaningful payload, and prune on a schedule. Anchorpoint does the third; GitLab locks make the first natural.

What is deliberately not in this report

Nothing here proposes a model where a deterministic reader works, nothing proposes the engine become a CAM system, and nothing proposes buying a PDM. Four frontier models shipped in three days and the recommendation for the engine is two model-string swaps that were already due, made on a replay so the live parallel run is not disturbed. The interesting technology this month sits at rank 40 or beside the engine, not inside its arbitration — and that is where it should stay.
