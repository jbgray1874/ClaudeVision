What moved since 16 September

The server is being ordered. On 16 September it was the one item on every critical path and it had not been ordered. The specification has since been settled and moved up to a Threadripper PRO platform. The reason is that the business has confirmed it will run several things at once on it: one estimate driving SolidWorks, one or two more estimates that do not need SolidWorks, Muhammad's search tool and Yogesh's Design Vault. It is being ordered from Scan 3XS with a three-week lead time. James Gray and Jack Calow, the admin owners, then need about a week to build it. That puts it in service in the week of 26 October. Section 05 has the full specification.

Three go-lives now share one date: the server's. AI estimating, Production Design Extraction (drawing packs into the production area for the estimators) and Drawing Search all go live when the server does, estimated at the end of October. That replaces the "September / October" estimating target and P2's 29 September date. In line with Matt's steer of 17 September, the parity evidence decides go-live, not the date: the server makes go-live possible, and the AI-against-manual comparisons in section 02 are what confirm it. Two pieces of work are being brought forward in parallel. Yogesh is working on the SolidWorks drawing automations, which were scheduled for January/February, as far as he can. Muhammad and James are analysing how to fast-track the creative design process by integrating automations with the search tool.

The estimating wave doubled. Twenty packs went out in September against eleven at the last report, and eight detailed reviews have come back from four estimators: Howard, Tim, Dave and Tony. The newest jobs are also the largest: a full-bay lit header kit at 163 off, a Tesco external shelter with two roller shutters, and a chiller shelf that estimating logged as three jobs. Dave read the printed 11650-06 book and wrote “This looks good now” across it.

Sage X3 changed shape for the better. Loading and user testing now happen in one cycle, one area at a time, on real SDI data. Estimating goes first, in the week of 5 October. All 813 source tables are classified, and every record in 8 of the 13 load templates now carries a status and a reason. Routes are in scope. The dress rehearsal and cutover move to November, to be confirmed.

Technical Design reached a decision point. The Design Vault's core loop (check-in, check-out, locking, versioning) works and was well received by the designers on 23 September. Yogesh has set a go/no-go at the end of October on Standard against in-house. The feature that decides it is cross-project and library referencing, and that is what he builds and demonstrates next.

Client Briefing went from two briefs to live jobs. Version 2 was built from the design team's 25 requests, with 19 done and 3 partly done. It now shows Jonathan's current jobs from his inbox, 24 of them today, alongside his calendar.

The lesson from 1176-02 is structural

A price the customer has already given us sits third in the pricing waterfall, but nothing in a drawing pack tells the engine it exists. It has to be entered somewhere, and the workbook needs a place for it. The same review also handed over two things the engine can adopt: the powder rate from Access Supply Chain (POWDER5, Jotun) and a hanging-geometry method for powder-coat throughput. Both are the next changes.

What landed in the engine

BOMs and Routes on every runNew · Tim

Tim asked on 16 September: “We are looking to Route & Bom this up as well.” Every run now writes a BOMs and Routes pair beside the estimate: a page to read and a workbook to filter. It prices nothing. It answers the two questions that come before any price: what parts are in the pack, and what work each one needs. Each row carries the drawing, page and reader it came from, and quantities are shown both as printed and as rolled up. It is also the natural input to the Sage X3 estimating load in section 06.

A pack is checked against its drawings before it is runNew · method

From 12614-01 onwards, each pack's drawings are read first and a pass table is written down: the parts list, folds, finishes, what is supplied by others. Each book is then scored against that table. When a book fails a row, the cause is traced and fixed in the engine and the pack is re-run. The book itself is never edited by hand.

Material is confirmed before the runNew · Dave

The estimator now confirms material and finish before the engine prices anything. 12645 was the first pack to use this step, with Dave's instruction for standard mild steel.

The sheet tells the truth about what is openNew · 12645

The banner at the top of the Estimate now counts every open item, not only the priced ones. That includes a bought-in item with no line at all, a stated row the product does not carry, provisional overflow steel and market figures, with the money at stake beside each. Department hours are read from every labour row. The template's own references were corrected, and an audit tool was added for earlier books.

Layout the estimators asked forDone

The Estimate sheet opens on values, with unused rows folded away (Howard). Parts priced in a material block are no longer repeated as £0 bill lines (Dave). Labour rows name their drawings in their own column, and header values no longer overwrite a label.

Run time is the next piece of engineeringNext

Large packs such as 12645 take too long end to end. Once this week's jobs are estimated, the work is to time each stage of a run and then re-run only the stages a change affects. The server in section 05 is the other half of the answer.

PDM Standard, pinned down with Ian29 Sep

The go/no-go hinges on cross-project referencing, so Yogesh asked Ian B exactly how PDM Standard behaves. The Vault has to match these answers:

  • One local root on every laptop, the same for everyone, for example C:\SDI-PDM\Projects\TTi\12689_…
  • A part owned by another project (the bracket from released L20, used in R100) is cached to the laptop as a read-only copy when the assembly opens, refreshed by Get Latest. Another designer can still check it out in L20.
  • Changing that part from inside R100: PDM offers to save a renamed copy (if the change is only for R100) or to check it out from L20, its owner.
  • If L20 is released: state change, new revision, make the change, re-release to production.
  • After check-in others see the new part only after Get Latest (a checkout gets latest automatically). Both projects still point at the one file in L20. A released Prototype or Production package does not pick up the change; it has to be published again.

Client Briefing: a big weekv2 · live jobs

The design team saw version 1 and liked it. They also said what to change: too many words on screen, and they wanted to see at a glance whether a job is big or small, how much information is in, and whether it is booked. They asked for a view per person, 2D design split from artwork, Jonathan's calendar in the tool, and a way to capture briefs that arrive by phone or WhatsApp. They agreed to start with technical.

Version 2 was built from that feedback and runs alongside version 1 so the two can be compared. Of the 25 things the team asked for, 19 are done and 3 partly done:

  • Every job shows a size (S/M/L), how much information is in, and whether it is booked. Whether a job can start comes from the crucial missing items, not a percentage.
  • Each person picks their own view (Jonathan, 3D, technical, 2D, artwork, strategy, account manager) and sees only their part.
  • Jonathan's three everyday e-mails are drafted for him on every job: chasing what is missing, booking the team, confirming it is booked. He still checks and sends them himself.
  • Anyone can ask about a job by typing or by voice, and the answer is read back.
  • Running on live jobs: the current jobs from Jonathan's inbox, 24 today, with new ones picked up automatically at no extra AI cost.
  • Jonathan's calendar, with his agreement, is in the tool read-only and refreshes every few minutes. It looks like his Outlook and shows who is free or away. He still books in Outlook.
  • Voice and WhatsApp. After a phone call anyone can speak or upload a voice note. It is transcribed on SDI's own machine, so there is no AI cost and the client is not recorded. The WhatsApp side is built and tested with sample messages. The next test uses a Meta test number, which needs IT to set up a secure connection; SDI would then get a proper business number.
  • A new approach underneath. E-mails are stored once and their key facts extracted once. Planner, writer and checker agents write the brief, and every AI call is logged. The new system has cost $2.92 in AI so far, and the old tool's spend is now logged too.
Nick and Andy have seen it and see a clear opportunity to adapt it for account management. The Client Briefing session with Acuity in section 06 is asked for so that this builds on what X3 already provides rather than duplicating it.

Why the specification moved from the September request

The 3 September request was a single Ryzen 9 9950X box at around £5,000 ex VAT, sized for the fastest single core. Since then the business has said it will run several things at once on this machine: an estimate driving SolidWorks, one or two more that do not need it, the search tool and the Vault. A Threadripper PRO keeps the same single-core boost for SolidWorks. It adds four times the memory bandwidth and PCIe lanes, and a socket that takes more cores later without replacing the machine. The order is about £10,963 ex VAT.

To settle during the build week

The Access Supply Chain database behind SDI Live moves to Sage X3. Since 16 September the approach has changed. Data loading and user acceptance testing are one cycle, area by area: each area is loaded into test, tested by its own users on real SDI data, fixed, and then kept current by incremental updates. That means UAT runs on the data SDI will actually go live with, and Acuity's standard reports list becomes the test scripts.

Estimating goes first

The first full pass is products, prices, BOMs and routes, loaded and user-tested end to end in X3 with Dave's team. It covers:

  • Functions today against X3. How Dave's team creates prices and imports BOMs and routes in Access, and the X3 equivalents. Dave's template is the starting point, and the aim is to improve on it, not copy it.
  • Pre-populated tables. The X3 tables that must exist before an estimate can be uploaded: product categories, units, work centres, labour and cost rates, price structures. Acuity has been asked for the list, with templates for any SDI supplies.
  • Loading SDI's data into test: current products, work centres, BOMs (lowest level first), routes and prices.
  • UAT on estimates. Sample estimates uploaded with their BOMs, routes and prices, viewed, and run through the standard reports. This includes how multi-level BOMs and routes are created and maintained in X3, and how the estimating tools connect.
  • Manufacturing actuals. Actual routes and times from production loaded back, so that estimated and actual can be compared.
  • Incremental updates. Only the changes from Access are loaded, not a full dump and reload, which keeps test current through to cutover.

Where this meets SDI Intelligence. The engine's BOMs and Routes output (section 02) is an estimate's BOM and route in a structured form, which is the shape an X3 upload takes. How the estimating tools connect is now an explicit UAT item, rather than something to solve after go-live.

What loads, and what stays behind

SDI expects to load roughly 40% of the product, BOM and route data and around 60% of customers and suppliers. The rest stays in the SDILive archive, which is taken in full, verified and queryable before Access is switched off.

Each record sits in one bucket, and each bucket has its own route to X3. Good records load straight away. Incomplete records are fixed in the mapping or by the owner in Access. Possible duplicates are merged or confirmed by the owner. Review records are kept or dropped by the owner from a list. Old records are dropped as a confirmed block. The good bucket of each area loads first and is tested while the owners work the other buckets, so test gets bigger and cleaner each week.
