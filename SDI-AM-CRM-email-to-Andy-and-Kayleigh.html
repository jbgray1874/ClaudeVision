What it does today

  • Ask it anything about your accounts. "What's due this week?", "What's overdue?", "Tell me about Barbour.", "What's my biggest job by value?", "What am I waiting on?"
  • Change things by speaking a normal sentence, several at once: "Set Barbour's status to live and the next step to chase the PO, due Friday."
  • It reads every change back before saving. "Two changes. Barbour: Status from Quoted to Live. Barbour: NEXT STEPS from Awaiting sign-off to Chase the PO. Shall I save them?" — and only a plain yes saves it.
  • A morning briefing by email at 07:50 each weekday: what is due, what has slipped, what is worth chasing. Its Listen link reads the same thing aloud.
  • Drive mode for hands-free use, and the same four operations inside ChatGPT — look up, propose, confirm, recent activity.

Two details that matter more than they sound. It matches client names by sound as well as spelling, because speech recognition mangles them — "barber" finds Barbour. And it reads dates back in words, "the fourteenth of November" rather than 11/14/2026, because a date read as numbers is easy to mishear and agree to.

Why it is safe to let it write

This is the part I would want to be asked about, so here it is plainly.

  • It only sees your own rows — the ones where you are the owner.
  • It reads the tracker as you, through your own Microsoft sign-in with MFA, so SharePoint's permissions apply as well as ours.
  • A save is one cell, written by Excel Online itself, so formulas recalculate and co-authoring sees it as if a person typed it. The app never uploads or replaces the workbook — that rule is what protects everything else.
  • It checks the row has not moved first. If somebody edited it while you were talking, the save is abandoned and you are told. That is what stops your change quietly overwriting theirs.
  • It refuses the owner column and any calculated cell — margin, for instance. If it cannot tell whether a cell holds a formula it still refuses, rather than flatten a calculation nobody notices for a month.
  • Every proposal, save, cancellation and refusal is journalled with who and when.

The actual question: is this SDI's CRM?

What exists is a working, used thing rather than a demonstration — which makes it a reasonable starting point for a CRM, and a bad thing to let grow by accident. It is built to evolve: the records live in the tracker today, and the same update service already supports a SharePoint List, which is what I would move to for anything beyond a two-person pilot. A List gives each project a permanent ID, a true version check on every save, per-item history and proper column validation. That is configuration and a one-off migration, not a rebuild.

What it is not yet is a CRM in the sense you would mean it: no opportunity pipeline as a first-class thing, no contact history beyond the tracker's own columns, no forecasting. Whether it should grow those, or whether SDI buys something and this becomes the voice and mobile layer on top of it, is exactly the decision I would like to put in front of you rather than answer by myself.

Sage X3 — the part that needs settling first

I know there is an appetite to join this up with Sage, and I agree with it. One thing has to be settled before any of it is built, and it is not technical:

Anything Sage owns has to become read-only here — or the two will quietly disagree and neither can be trusted. A CRM and an ERP holding the same field, both editable, is the single most reliable way to end up with two versions of a number and an argument about which is right.

So the useful thing I could do with twenty minutes of your time: go down the tracker's columns — CLIENT, JOB, STATUS, NEXT STEPS, KEY DATES, BUDGET COST, CONFIDENCE, MARGIN, and the rest — and mark each one ours, Sage's, or both.

Where you say both, tell me who gets the last word when they disagree. That one exercise decides most of the integration design, and without it anything I build is a guess.

Fair warning on timing. X3 is still loading area by area and nothing for sales exists to read yet. So this is a "what should it look like" conversation now, and a build later — not something I can deliver this month, and I would rather say that than imply otherwise.

What is honestly not built

  • Per-person queues. It serves one owner today. Kayleigh's and Ian's queues are the next build item, along with setting up their access.
  • A permanent project ID. Rows have no fixed identity, so a row is found by position plus a fingerprint of its contents. That is safe — a mismatch refuses the save rather than writing to the wrong place — but it is not right for the long run.
  • Separate dated columns for production complete, delivered to stock, delivered to customer and invoice date. Today those live inside Status and notes, which means the stages cannot be told apart reliably, and any reporting built on them inherits that.

It is running as a sandbox pilot against a copy of the tracker, not the live one, and today only Nick and I can change anything — everyone else can ask it things. The screenshots in the guide use sample accounts and figures, not live data.
