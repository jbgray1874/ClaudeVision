Thank you — four points, and each one lands on something real. I have gone through them against the current build rather than answering from memory, so what follows is either the calculation that produced the figure you saw, or a plain statement that the gap is ours.

Two of the four are now changed on the engine and will show on the re-run. Two need a number from you — they are listed at the end so they are easy to answer in one go.

1 · Where does the engine get the rate for pemming?

Two figures, and they come from two different places. Neither is invented, and one of them is yours.

The rate is £31.18 an hour — the MANM department rate from the labour rates table. It is not a per-job figure and nothing on this drawing changed it.

The time is 15 seconds per pressed fastener, and that is your own 12120 sheet read back: four clinch nuts a unit at 60 units an hour, and two PEM studs a unit at 120 units an hour. Both come to 240 inserts an hour, so both say 15 seconds an insert.

4 PEM studs × 27 off  =  108 inserts
108 × 15 s  =  1,620 s  =  0.45 h run
+ 15 min MANM set-up  =  0.25 h
———————————————————
0.70 h × £31.18  =  £21.83 the batch  =  £0.81 a unit

That is the MANM row on your sheet, to the penny. Because the 15 seconds came off one sheet of yours, the engine holds it as a pilot rather than a shop constant — it is labelled as such, and if it is wrong, or if a clinch nut and a PEM stud should differ, your number replaces it.

2 · Where is the powder cost coming from for RAL 9003?

No colour is priced. RAL 9003 is not looked up at all — the engine prices powder by weight, and the colour never enters the sum:

coated area 0.1682 m² × 0.20 kg/m²  =  0.03364 kg
0.03364 kg × £4.00/kg  =  £0.14 a unit

The £4.00 a kilo is our configured rate for standard powder, and it is recorded as confirmed by estimating with your name against it — POWDER5 on job 1282. Metallic, textured and wrinkle specials carry their own higher rate; a standard RAL like 9003 takes the standard one. Since you are asking where it comes from, it is worth you confirming it still holds.

The second figure in that sum needs you as well. We use 0.20 kg/m², which is a derivation at about 50% transfer efficiency — what actually gets consumed. The workbook template's own Powder Qty Calculator uses 0.1667, which is 6 m² a kilo and assumes every particle lands. They are both defensible and they disagree by a fifth. Yours to pick.

Your suggestion about putting the powder reference on the drawing is the right fix, and I have raised it. With a powder reference on the pack the line prices from an actual SKU instead of a per-kilo average, which removes both of the questions above rather than answering them once.

3 · The fixings — is the spec missing from the drawing?

No. The spec on the drawing is fine — "M4 WING NUT" and "M4X8 PEM STUD" is enough to buy from. The failure is ours, and it is worth explaining because the sheet hid it from you.

Those two items carry no part number on the pack. So the engine mints a placeholder from the description — BI-NUT, BI-PEMSTUD — so that the line can be carried, counted and shown to you at all. That placeholder then sits in the code column of every document looking exactly like a part number. Nothing can be looked up against a code we wrote ourselves, so the chain falls back to matching your description against the purchasing catalogue, and that came back with nothing. All you saw was "NOT PRICED — needs a rate", which reads as a lookup we forgot.

Changed. An unpriced line whose code we minted now says so in as many words: that the code is ours, that the description was matched against the catalogue instead, and that the drawing's spec is not the problem. You will see it on the re-run.

On "off system": the shelf it asks is empty. No supplier price list has been loaded into the purchasing catalogue yet — Elite, Eagle and Thermaset are the three we need, and they are the standing item on every status report. Until one is in, a bought line with a perfect spec still finds nothing.

On "or internet": there is a web rung, and it is deliberately capped below a firm price so it can never be presented as a quote. It will not fill these lines and should not.

4 · The ticket strip — can it not take a previous cost off the system?

This one is the opposite case to the fixings, and the difference matters. DBR60 is a real code, so it was asked — by code, against the purchasing catalogue and the price history. Neither holds a price for it. That is a gap on our side, not a fault in the drawing.

Changed. The line now says that the code was put to both and that neither carries a rate, instead of "needs a rate" — so you can tell at a glance which of the two problems you are looking at.

One thing the drawing does affect: it gives the length, 758 mm, but not the material or the thickness for this item. So even a rate per metre could not be applied without knowing what it is made of.

On the previous cost specifically — historical comparables are a rung in the chain, ranked below the catalogue. Whether a previous DBR60 line exists to be found is in the run's own log, and I will confirm that on the re-run rather than guess at it here.
