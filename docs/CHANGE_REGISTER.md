# SDI Estimating Intelligence — decision & change register

**Adding a row is part of making the change** — an estimator response quotes its rows, so
the estimators always know what changed and where the change lives.

This document has two layers, and the distinction is the point of it.

> **Why it was split (16 Sep 2026).** Read as one list, this register claimed several rules
> were finished that the day's live workbooks disproved — a title fix that never reached the
> sheet, a price check still blocking, an answers file still not landing. A change register
> that records intentions as outcomes is worse than none, because it is exactly the document
> somebody reaches for when deciding whether a number can be trusted. So **Part 1 is what the
> engine is actually doing today**, and nothing enters it until a real book shows it working.
> Part 2 keeps every finding, attempt and correction, including the ones that failed.

## Part 1 — Current operating guide

Only rules **proven on a live book**. A rule written and tested but not yet seen working
stays in Part 2 until a workbook shows it.

**Rule types.** `safeguard` — a universal check that refuses or flags, never prices.
`engine rule` — a costing rule that fires on stated conditions. `customer term` — commercial
terms for one customer. `shop method` — a method or rate the shop stated. `job answer` — one
job's confirmed reading, governing that job only.

**A rate taken from a single estimate is a `scoped pilot`, never `generic`**, until a second
independent job or the department itself confirms it. That distinction is what D-045 got
wrong and is why this table carries the basis and the scope in their own columns.

### Mandatory rule for changes

Before merging any pricing, routing, extraction or workbook change, record:

- the generic behaviour being introduced or corrected;
- its evidence and source authority;
- applicable scope and explicit exclusions;
- whether it is proven, provisional, job-only or superseded;
- the test/replay that protects it;
- every customer-facing explanation or workbook surface affected.

A commit is not a completed learning change until this register is updated. A rule found only
in code, a test, a chat message or an old workbook is not operational knowledge.

### Price and method separation

Methods may become reusable rules when evidenced: for example, how roll goods are consumed,
when a process applies, or how setup is allocated.

Prices are data, not hidden logic. A price must state its source, date, scope, status and
review/expiry date. A job-specific quote must never become an automatic shared rate.
Historical prices may be used only as identified, labelled historical inference; live SDI,
supplier or current-job evidence takes precedence.

### No silent gaps

The engine must attempt to price every required item. Where the drawing is incomplete, it must
use the best available evidenced basis and explain it. "Not priced" is permitted only after the
documented source hierarchy has been exhausted, and must name the missing information and the
requested owner.

### Historic estimates teach; they do not supply prices

Historic estimates may teach the engine how to identify an item, route it, choose an
appropriate source, or recognise that a fresh quote is required. They must not supply a
numeric price to a new estimate unless that number is independently revalidated from its
original current source.

A figure from an earlier job may not appear in a new estimate as a charge, a fallback, a
comparator, a workbook note or a suggested value. Where one is retained for audit it lives
outside the pricing resolver, and no estimator run may read, display or charge it.

### Non-negotiable pricing policy

**A number copied from an estimator's sheet is not a price source, even as a "reference".** It must not be retained in the current register, documentation, reports, prompts, tests, or audit payloads. What carries forward is the method, applicability, SKU/specification, route and evidence — not the number. A line the engine cannot price from SDI Live / UDEF, a supplier catalogue or API, or an identified current quote is raised awaiting price, naming the owner. (James Gray, 16 Sep 2026; D-078.)

**The engine learns methods and evidence; it does not copy an old estimate into a new one.**
Code may decide *how* to price—quantity, yield, route, setup allocation and applicability—but a
job-specific amount must not become an automatically chargeable code constant.

Prices are resolved and printed in this order, with the winning source, date and scope shown
on the line:

1. **Exact current SDI Live / UDEF purchase or SKU price.**
2. **Exact current supplier catalogue or connected supplier API price.**
3. **A current quote or confirmed price for this job.**
4. **A matched historical SDI estimate**, identified by job, date, supplier/specification and
   match basis. It may price a new job as a *historical comparator/inference*, never as an
   unlabelled standing rate.
5. **LLM market research/estimate** when no better source exists. It is still a price, but
   must be labelled indicative and retain its evidence/date so an estimator can replace it.

The drawing, DXF and SolidWorks files determine the required item, geometry and route; they
do not turn a supplier quote from another job into a current price. A named finish may identify
the process, but does not by itself inherit a previous job's amount. Where the source is old,
missing or inferred, the estimate still prices the work and explains that basis rather than
silently omitting it.

### Configuration and pricing logic

**Configuration is SDI's named knowledge register, not a second estimating engine.**

A value belongs in configuration only when it is reusable, named and evidenced: for example a
material family, stocked sheet size, supplier/SKU mapping, department rate, approved routing
method, packing rule, customer commercial term, or a dated stated figure. Each entry must carry
a meaningful variable/key name plus source, owner, date, scope and review status.

Pricing calculations and decision logic must be centralised in the engine's pricing modules.
The configuration supplies the facts and rules they consume; it must not hide bespoke
calculation code or silently embed a prior job's price.

The estimate workbook remains authoritative for its own formulas, template layout and visible
quantity-break calculations. The engine must populate it with traceable inputs and read back
the resulting figures; it must not recreate divergent spreadsheet logic in configuration or
code.

A new pricing-related setting must answer all of these:

- What does this value represent, in plain estimator language?
- Is it a reusable rule, a current price, a historic comparator, or a job-only answer?
- What source supports it, and when was that source current?
- Which materials, departments, customers, products or job families may use it?
- What supersedes it, and how is a disagreement shown to the estimator?

Job-specific confirmations and quotes remain beside that job's drawings. They must never be
promoted to shared configuration merely because they produced a good result once.
