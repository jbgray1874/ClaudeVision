◆ This week's specific ask — delivered

Amend a sheet, upload it, get a new quote Built & Working

Owner · James Gray  ·  Requested by · Dave

The request was for “an area to the intelligence tool to ensure when they manually modify a prepopulated sheet, they have an area to reupload and regenerate a new quote ready for next week.” That is built, tested and on the portal.

  • The estimator's own figure is the one that goes out. The sheet is read for Sell Price in preference to Unit Cost, so a margin they set is what the customer sees.
  • Hours or days later is fine. They re-enter units, drawing number and client; nothing needs re-running and no drawings are needed.
  • The full quotation is preserved — material, finish, operations and the GA image come from the original run, with only the money and those three fields overlaid.
  • Their sheet is kept as the record, filed as <job>_MANUAL_OVERRIDE.xlsx alongside the quote on the AI Estimating share.
  • One operational note for training: the workbook must be opened and saved in Excel first. The totals are formulas, and Excel only stores the computed values on save. If it has not been, the tool says so rather than producing a blank quote.
Why this matters for Week One. The 24–28 August plan has estimators working from pre-populated sheets and sending their final versions back for quoting. That loop now exists as a page rather than a developer task, which is what makes the week runnable.

⌬ SOLIDWORKS API — the fortnight's largest piece of engineering

Reading the model itself, not a picture of it Built · Proving blocked

Owner · James Gray  ·  Plan tasks 20, 26

The bulk of development and testing over the past two weeks has gone into integrating the native SOLIDWORKS COM API into the estimating pipeline. This is the difference between inferring a part's material and gauge from a printed drawing and reading them from the model the shop actually builds from. It is the single largest accuracy step available to us, and it has been the hardest to build — not because the API is difficult to call, but because a CAD application is a live, stateful, licensed piece of software that a designer may be using at the same moment the engine wants to read from it.

Scoped on evidence, not on the brochure. Before a line of production code was written, a read-only discovery probe opened a batch of SDI's own models over the full API and produced a coverage matrix — for how many models is material actually populated? Cut lists? Flat patterns? Surface area? That answered which capabilities were worth building against and which were promises the files could not keep. Phase scope followed the matrix.

What the connector reads, and the rule for each

DatumRule — and why it is not simply "take the model's word"
MaterialFills a gap always. Overrides the drawing only where the family is wrong (metal ↔ non-metal). A same-family grade disagreement is flagged and the drawing kept — the title block is what Purchasing buys to.
Flat blank, gauge,
bend radius		From the sheet-metal cut list. Written only where the part is not already DXF-backed; where it is, the two blanks are compared and a disagreement over 10% by area is flagged, with the measured DXF kept.
BendsFrom the feature tree. Where a Base Flange bakes its bends into the sketch, the part is flagged formed, bend count unreadable rather than silently counted flat.
Weldment / tube
cut list		New this fortnight. Section profile and member length parsed from cut-list properties, so a welded frame is priced from its real bar sections instead of being absent. Needs no additional API call — the existing enumeration already read these properties and was discarding them.
QuantitiesFrom the full-depth assembly BOM, which is structural fact rather than a reading of a printed table.
AssembliesMarked as parents so their material is never costed twice alongside their children — the GA double-count rule.
Imported bodiesA supplier body with no modelled fabrication is marked bought-in and takes no fabrication route.
Nothing derivableFlag, never zero. If the model names a material but yields no blank, mass or section, the part is flagged cost not derivable. A £0 line must never read as "free".

The hard parts — where the two weeks actually went

  • Never touch a designer's open work. The analyser opens documents read-only and closes only the documents it opened; anything already open is read in place, left open, and recorded in the manifest. An earlier version used a blanket close that shut everything it touched — that hazard is removed, and it is why the in-pipeline call had been switched off for weeks while jobs with models sitting beside them were quietly costed from PDF alone.
  • Session and integrity-level binding. COM attaches within a Windows session and at a matching integrity level. An elevated console cannot take over a SOLIDWORKS a designer already has open. This is precisely why the server needs its own seat — a licence in use on somebody's desktop cannot serve the engine.
  • Old jobs re-read when the code improves. The extract now carries a schema version. Previously an extract was only refreshed when the models changed, so a 2019 or 2025 pack kept a stale read for ever and every reader improvement bypassed it. A fix now reaches every job, not only the ones being redrawn.
  • Self-gating, so it can never break a normal run. The connector fires when an extract is present and does nothing when it is not — no seat, no models, no effect; the job runs on PDF and DXF exactly as before, and says which it used.
  • Known limits, measured from the code rather than the documentation: a 30-minute analyser timeout in-pipeline (41 models is comfortable, several hundred is not); one instance; a 1–2500 mm flat-blank window outside which the model's blank is refused and the part falls back — now flagged, where it used to be silent.
  • Proving is blocked on the licence, not on engineering. The tube/weldment reader is written and tested offline. It needs a seat and a pack with models — the Dyson 10575-01 component packs — to be demonstrated end to end.

▤ Architecture — how a drawing pack becomes a price

The flow, and the merge model that arbitrates between sources Reference

Source waterfall · arbitration · canonical BOM and route · deliverables

Every source that can say something about a part is read, and none of them is trusted blindly. Each carries a rank. The rule is absolute and it is the reason the engine can be audited: a weaker source may FILL a datum, it may never REPLACE a stronger one — and where the two disagree, the disagreement is written onto the part and the stronger value kept. Nothing is decided by whichever pass happens to run last.

Upscaling the drawings put to xAI In progress

Engine · vision path  ·  Owner · James Gray

The vision fallback is only as good as the image it is given, and that is where it has been losing information. Pages are rendered at 300 DPI, but the image put to xAI is then capped at 2000 pixels on the long side to control cost. On an A4 detail that cap never binds. On an A1 general arrangement it binds hard — 2000 px across an 841 mm sheet is an effective resolution around 60 DPI, at which a 2.5 mm BOM digit and a material callout are simply not legible. The local OCR path already runs to a 4000 px cap; the paid vision path was the one still throttled.

What changes. The drawings put to xAI are upscaled — the cap raised so a large sheet reaches the model at a resolution its BOM and callouts can actually be read at, with dense sheets handled region-by-region rather than squeezed whole into one frame. The purpose is narrow and deliberate: better recovery of BOM rows, route operations and bought-in identification for pricing — the three things the vision layer exists to backstop.
  • It changes what is recovered, not who wins. The vision layer stays at rank 40. A sharper image lets it fill more gaps; it still cannot overwrite a model or a measured DXF.
  • Cost is the trade, and it is bounded. A larger image costs more per call, so it applies to the sheets where the cap actually binds. Every vision result is cached per page, so a re-run of a job — including every estimator re-issue — is free.
  • Re-priming the cache is part of the change — existing cached reads were taken at the old resolution and have to be re-taken for a job to benefit.
  • Measured, not assumed. The test is whether BOM rows recovered by vision alone rise, and whether flagged disagreements fall, on packs already run at the old cap.

Of nineteen issued over fifteen weeks, twelve drew no response at all. And of the seven responses received, only one recorded what was actually changed at the level of a number — the 1310 Drill Stud Holder, where powder coating, welding and one measurement difference were raised and integrated. Four more were logged as "various improvements suggested" with the detail not captured. The programme's most valuable dataset is being received and not written down.
  • Zero responses since 28 July, across five estimates issued in that time.
  • One formal sign-off in fifteen weeks, and it was the first job issued.
  • Coverage gap unchanged — TTi and hand-drawn packs have still not been run with an estimator. Hand-drawn remains recommended for explicit exclusion from v1.
  • The agreed parallel-run target was roughly twenty jobs a week across two reviewers. The actual rate since 5 May is 1.3 issued per week and 0.5 answered.
  • From the next issue onward the log gains one column — the estimator's figure against the engine's, wherever a number was changed. That is what turns adoption tracking into engine tuning, and it is the reason the feedback return route is worth eight hours of anyone's time.
  • Issue to a named person, not to a pair. Seven of the nineteen went to two names at once and only one of those seven drew a response — 14%. Of the twelve issued to a single named person, six were answered — 50%. Shared ownership is measurably no ownership.
