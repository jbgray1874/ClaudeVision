## What will go wrong on the current engine, and what to look for

- **"BLOB WELD" was not in the weld-note vocabulary** (TAC WELD, WELD & DRESS, WELDED, WELD BOTH
  SIDES were). Added before this run (D-400), with WELD BOTH ENDS and WELD EACH END. Expect the
  weld on 01M at drawing_deterministic, quoting the note, with "MIG per the pack's weld
  specification" on the row. If the weld is "inferred, not drawn", the note was not read.
- **The weights are zero and nonsense.** 0.00 g on the frame must not price steel by stated
  weight and must not fail the blank-weight check; 3.66 g on the GA must not be reconciled against
  the parts. Expect the frame costed from its blank and gauge, and no weight question.
- **The 180° hem.** A reader that counts only 90° callouts, or only DXF bend lines, under-counts.
  Expect a fold count with its source named (sheet callouts, model bend features, DXF), and a
  disagreement published if they differ. The engine must not route Linebend: this is steel.
- **The graphic's material "400 MIC" resolves to nothing in the lexicon**, so the name rule makes
  it bought (D-399: no stock material on its sheet). Expect one bought/asked line, not a laser or a
  sheet of steel, and a question for Tim on who prints it. Note the `-G` suffix: if SDI's numbering
  means graphic by it as it means M, A, T and X, that is a convention to add, not a job rule.
- **The bumpers' code** FIXING1270 is a catalogue family code and must price from the parts
  table; if it does not, the line is NOT PRICED and asked, never a near match.
- **The GA must be the product** from the drawing number 9598-02 typed on the portal (D-395,
  D-398), with 01M, 02G and FIXING1270 under it and nothing set aside.

## First run — 6 Oct 14:15 book on e21761e (£47.38 a unit at 20 off; material £17.09, labour £25.72, margin £4.58)

**Not a handover price.** The one engine fault accounts for about £20.52 of the £47.38: the graphic
`02G` was charged as an **18 mm TIMBER panel, 841 × 471.61** — £9.19 of board on the Other Sheet
block and £9.34 of CNC Joinery — on a 400 micron printed card whose own sheet states 210 × 148.5 ×
0.40 and 1.95 g. Three readers overrode the part's own field and its own model:

- **Material.** The labelled "MATERIAL: 400 MIC" was refused by the keyword list, so the reader
  scanned the whole page and took TIMBER from the legend's "WHERE SPECIFIED ALL TIMBER-BASED
  PRODUCTS MUST BE FSC CERTIFIED" (with STAINLESS STEEL and ALUMINIUM from "• 304 - STAINLESS
  STEEL" and "• 6063 - ALUMINIUM FOR EXTRUSION"). Two sources then "agreed" on TIMBER against the
  model's Corrugated Paper. Reproduced on the pack's own pdfplumber text.
- **Gauge.** The model's 0.4 mm was refused below the 6 mm timber floor, and the family default
  (18 for TIMBER) had been written into the drawing's thickness list, where the estimator read it
  back as the drawing's reading. The gauge advisory said "costed at 0.4" beside a row nested at 18.
- **Blank.** With no flat pattern and no DXF, the blank came from the two largest numbers in the
  document text: 841 (the sheet) and 471.61 (the frame's flat length). The model's own extents
  (210 × 148.5 × 0.4) were on the record and unused.

Fixed as D-402 (generic): a labelled MATERIAL value the lexicon does not know is still the sheet's
