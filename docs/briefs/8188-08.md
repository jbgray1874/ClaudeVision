## What will go wrong, and what to look for

- **The swing stopper.** Its PDF is in the folder; it was removed at Rev G and is on no parts list. Any
  8188-12 line on the book is a scope fault.
- **The drawing number is printed "8188-08_GA"** (underscore), the file says "8188-08-GA". Type
  **8188-08** as the Drawing Number; the book should resolve the one top assembly under it. If the
  first line says the number "matches no assembly", that is the known wording, not a scope failure —
  check the book prices the GA.
- **The stated weights on the acrylic parts are not credible.** The "FISHMONGER" letters say 4.956 kg
  and wave layer 013 11.731 kg — several times what 10 mm and 3 mm acrylic of those sizes weigh. The
  blank-weight check must not turn them into a heavier gauge or another material: the gauges are on the
  sheets (10, 6 and 3 mm). The steel weights are sound (the base checks to the gram).
- **Two parts with no sheet of their own:** the 6 mm wire frame (004) and the mesh (009). Expect them
  as "no sheet of its own"; the mesh is bought by the panel, the frame is wire bent and welded — sized
  from the grill sheet (1081 × 206).
- **Laser or CNC on the waves.** The GA note says "LASERED 3MM ACRYLIC" and the title block "LASERED
  EDGES"; the layer sheet says "CNC IN TWO HALVES". At about 2.4 m each layer is longer than the laser
  bed, which may be why it is cut in halves. A question for Howard, not a guess.
- **Wave layer 013's colour:** the layer sheet says Pantone 290U; its title block says 7691U.
- **Weld processes:** MIG on the common frame (its own legend), TIG on the GA's parts, and only where a
  sheet states a weld (the goalpost corners, the mesh tacked all round, the frame to the base). The
  legend alone is never a weld.
- **Food-safe powder** on both steel coats; check the P.Coat rows name the right colour per part.
- **Edging:** paint-grade tape on all edges of the MDF panel — metres from its perimeter.
- **Quantity breaks** 1 / 5 / 10 / 50 on one workbook; bought-in prices do not step down.
