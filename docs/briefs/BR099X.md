## The right book

- **Steel, folded and powder coated:** six slotted corner posts (lasered slots, folded), the top-hat
  spine, top cap (with its slot), three headers, rear graphic panel, base plinth, two graphic holders,
  fifteen shelf trays (five front, ten side, the side ones handed) each with two hooked brackets —
  welded or riveted on, one route chosen and named.
- **One neutral powder coat** over the whole unit.
- **Assembly and pack:** flat-packed for SEC Castleford, "minimal on-site assembly"; packaging and
  delivery to SEC as lines.
- **Not in it:** graphics, card inserts and mag-graphics ("All display aids are being managed
  separately"); lighting and digital (out of scope); development, drawings, prototypes, test rigs,
  installation guides, O&M — the brief asks for these as separate costings, outside the book.

## What will go wrong, and what to look for

- **The podium.** Slide 3's SOTF Podium (501 × 451, Beauty/Photo/Baby variants) is not this unit.
  Any line sized from it, or a footprint of 501 × 451, is the wrong page in the pack.
- **The height and the gauges are assumptions.** The book must say so on the unit and in the banner;
  a figure that reads as checked is wrong.
- **Shelf brackets as separate parts.** The brief says the hooked brackets are welded or riveted to
  the tray, not separate; two per shelf, thirty in all, and the fixing is a route, not a bought-in.
- **Handed side shelves.** Ten side shelves, five of each hand — not ten of one part, not twenty.
- **Graphics priced.** Card and mag-graphics are Boots' own; a print line is out of scope.
- **Quantity basis.** The brief prices at 200; the book's unit cost at 1 off is a prototype figure,
  setup-dominated.

## What the Boots standard says (BFDG v6, June 2026)

The second Boots file is not a product: it is *Boots In-Store Equipment Design Standards (BFDG) V6*,
101 pages, which the brief says every component must conform to. What bears on this estimate:

- **Stability, 3.3.3 and 4.6.1:** "a 1:3 ratio (base to height) may be applied as a starting point,
  but this is not a hard and fast requirement". A 500 base gives about 1,500 mm; the drawing scales
  at about 2,100 (1:4.2). Free-standing units must not topple at 15° (BTC001, items 1,067 mm or
  taller); a low centre of gravity within the footprint; "tall items with narrow base-widths are
  intrinsically unstable" — larger base plates, stage weights or a heavy base to be investigated.
- **Base plate no thicker than 5 mm** (trip hazard).
- **Levellers whenever possible**, 6 mm reveal retracted, top adjustment — four are on the take-off.
- **Display plinth at least 200 mm** (4.1.10) — the take-off's 220 mm meets it.
- **Shelves, BTC006/007:** loaded at 1.5 kg per 100 × 100 mm for an hour, deflection at most 0.5% of
  the width — on a 420 × 180 front tray about 11 kg and 2.1 mm. The 1.2 mm tray with its folded lips
  is the assumption to prove.
- **No sharp edges:** everything rounded, chamfered or deburred; corner protection to be considered.
- **Testing to section 5 is the supplier's** — a development cost, outside the book.

**The height decision (9 Oct): 1,500 mm, assumed.** It meets the BFDG's 1:3 guide; the drawing
scales at about 2,100, which would need ballast or a wider base to pass BTC001 and about 12 kg more
steel. Run at 1,500; a second run at 2,100 gives the other figure if Boots confirm the taller unit.
