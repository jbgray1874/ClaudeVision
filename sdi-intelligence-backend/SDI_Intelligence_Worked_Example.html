SDI Intelligence — Worked Example

How the engine processes a real job end to end  ·  Flat-Pack Trestle  ·  11 June 2026
About this example: This walks through a real engine run on an actual SDI job — a flat-pack trestle with mixed timber, mild steel and MDF parts. The engine produced a suppressed result on this job (44% credible) because only 7 of 17 parts have individual DXFs. That makes it an ideal example — it shows both what the engine gets right and exactly why and how it suppresses when it doesn't have enough information.

Step 1 — What Goes In

The estimator (or James) points the engine at a job folder on the K: drive. The folder contains the SolidWorks drawing pack — a GA drawing plus individual part drawings, some as DXF flat patterns, some as PDFs only.

Step 2 — How the Engine Classifies Each Part

Before any pricing, the engine must determine what each part is made of and how it is manufactured. It reads material and thickness from three sources in priority order: the DXF filename → the PDF drawing → inference from part number patterns.

What ✅ means — high confidence source
The engine found explicit material information in the DXF filename or part number — a naming convention SDI uses consistently. These decisions are reliable.
What ⚡ means — AI inference
No explicit token found. The engine inferred material from description text, drawing context or assembly structure. These are lower confidence and worth checking on the Decision Report.
What ⚠ means — data missing
A required value (usually thickness) couldn't be determined. The engine uses a tolerance-table fallback. Flag for manual check if the part is high-cost.

Step 4 — Why This Job Was Suppressed

The engine checks credibility before printing a headline total. On this job, only 7 of 17 parts have DXF flat patterns — 41% coverage. The threshold for a reliable estimate is ~60%. The engine suppresses and explains exactly why.

What to do with a suppressed result
Request individual part DXFs from the drawing office for the parts listed above. Once those DXFs are added to the job folder and the engine is re-run, credibility will rise above 60% and the headline will be released. The parts that are already correct (the ✅ DXF-backed ones) do not need to be re-done.

Step 5 — Reading the Output Workbook

The five-sheet workbook is the deliverable. Here is what to look at on each sheet and in what order.

SheetOpen it when…Key things to check
1. EstimateFirst — alwaysRow 5: unit cost and quantity. Row 7: suppression banner (if present). Bottom: cost breakdown by material type. If row 7 shows ⚠ INSUFFICIENT DATA, do not use the unit cost in row 5 for quoting.
2. LabourWhen labour looks high or lowDepartment totals at the top — check weld time, CNC time, laser time feel right for the job complexity. Then the per-part per-operation detail below.
3. Material Price BreakWhen material cost looks wrongPrice per part, supplier, price date. Check bought-in items especially — Wanzl/UPC prices may need updating if the price date is old.
4. Decision ReportWhen anything looks wrongThe engine's reasoning — material, thickness, operations and WHY for every part. This is the first place to look when a number seems off. The confidence column and flags column tell you which parts to focus on.
5. AI ProvenanceFor audit / deep investigationFull audit trail — geometry source, price source, rate source per part. Rate/source column (col N) shows exactly where each price came from: Config rate card / Price book / Historical RAG / ⚠ No price source.

Step 6 — What the Estimator Does With It

The engine's output is a starting point, not a final answer. The estimator's job is to review it, correct what's wrong, and use their knowledge of SDI's capabilities and the customer's requirements to arrive at a quotable number.

  1. Check the suppression banner first. If suppressed — is the reason valid? If the job has DXFs you know exist, check the folder structure and re-run. If it's genuinely no-DXF, the engine is right to suppress.
  2. Check the Decision Report for red flags. Any part flagged ⚡ AI inference on material, or ⚠ on thickness, is worth a second look. Cross-check against the drawing title block.
  3. Check the headline variance. If the engine is within 15% of your manual estimate on a DXF-backed steel job — that's a pass. If it's outside 15%, use the Decision Report to find which line is driving the gap.
  4. Log any corrections. Part number, field, what the engine said, what it should be. Hand to James. The correction goes into the learning engine and fires automatically on future jobs.
Remember: the engine knows what it doesn't know. A suppressed result is the engine being honest — it is not a failure. A confident wrong number would be far more dangerous. If the engine suppresses and the reason is valid, trust it and get the missing DXFs.
